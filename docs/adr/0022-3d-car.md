# 0022 The car is drawn in 3D from a generic body for its kind, painted the owner's color

Status: accepted, 2026-10-18

## Decision

The Car screen shows the owner's car as an interactive 3D model they can turn, with service items
and recalls placed where they are on the car.

- **One generic body per kind of car**, not a model of the exact car. Licensed per-model 3D
  libraries are quote-only and priced for dealers, and stock models are licensed for editorial use
  only, so they can't ship in an app. The app draws four bodies (sedan, SUV, pickup, minivan) in
  code, proportioned from real cars of each kind.
- **Body style from the VIN** when vPIC's `BodyClass` names one of the four; coupes, wagons and
  hatchbacks draw as the sedan. Otherwise, or if the VIN is wrong about it, the owner picks it on
  the Edit car sheet, which is also where they pick the paint from eight common car colors. Both
  are optional columns on `vehicles`; until set the app draws an SUV in silver.
- **Paint colors are content, not roles** (an exception to ADR 0007): `paintColors` in the theme maps
  each paint to one fixed swatch, the same in light and dark, since it is the car's color and not
  what a color means in the UI. The swatches still live in the palette.
- **Optional.** 3D is a per-device setting the owner can turn off, and it is off by default under
  Reduce Motion, Low Power Mode and on phones too old to draw it smoothly. Off, the Car screen shows
  a still side view of the same body. Everything the model shows is also in the Service list, so
  the 3D view is never the only way to reach anything.
- **Additive.** The 3D view is loaded lazily and sits at the top of the Car screen; the rest of the
  app and its data are unchanged apart from the two new columns.

## Revisit when

A licensed library of per-model 3D cars becomes affordable for consumer apps, or owners ask for a
kind of car the four bodies don't cover (vans, sports cars).
